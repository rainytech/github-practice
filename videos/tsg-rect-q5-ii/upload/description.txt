T.S. Grewal Class 11 (CBSE, 2025–26 edition) · Chapter 17: Rectification of Errors · Question 5 (ii) · Page 17.44
Error: Salary paid to an employee, Ajay, is debited to his Personal Account ₹8,000.

✅ RECTIFYING JOURNAL ENTRY
Salary A/c … Dr. 8,000
   To Ajay's A/c 8,000
(Being salary paid to Ajay wrongly debited to his Personal Account, now rectified)

📘 THE 4-STEP METHOD (works for every rectification question)
STEP 1 — Wrong entry (what was done):
Ajay's A/c … Dr. 8,000
   To Cash A/c 8,000

STEP 2 — Reverse the wrong entry (swap Dr. and Cr.):
Cash A/c … Dr. 8,000
   To Ajay's A/c 8,000

STEP 3 — Correct entry (what should have been done):
Salary A/c … Dr. 8,000
   To Cash A/c 8,000

STEP 4 — Rectifying entry = Step 2 + Step 3
Cash A/c is debited in Step 2 and credited in Step 3, so Cash cancels out.
What remains: Salary A/c Dr. 8,000 To Ajay's A/c 8,000 ✅

💡 WHY?
Cash was recorded correctly — the money really was paid.
Only the debit went to the wrong account: Ajay's Personal Account instead of Salary A/c (an expense).
So we move the debit from Ajay's A/c to Salary A/c.
Both sides were affected equally, so the Trial Balance still agrees — no Suspense A/c is needed.

⚠️ COMMON MISTAKES
✗ Writing "Salary A/c Dr. To Cash A/c" — that records the payment a second time.
✗ Using Suspense A/c — not needed, because the error affects both sides equally.
✗ Forgetting the narration "…now rectified".
