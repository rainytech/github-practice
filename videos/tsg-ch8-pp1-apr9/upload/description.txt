T.S. Grewal Class 11 (CBSE, 2025–26 edition) · Chapter 8: Journal · Practical Problem 1 · Page 8.57
Transaction: 2023, April 9 — Goods returned by Naresh ₹1,400

✅ JOURNAL ENTRY
Date: 2023, April 9
Sales Return A/c … Dr. 1,400
   To Naresh A/c 1,400
(Being goods returned by Naresh)

📘 STEP-BY-STEP EXPLANATION
On April 5, the business sold goods to Naresh on credit for ₹9,000 — so Naresh owed us ₹9,000.
On April 9, Naresh sends back goods worth ₹1,400.
1️⃣ Goods COME BACK into the business from the customer → this is a SALES RETURN (also called RETURN INWARD).
2️⃣ Naresh GIVES the goods back → his account is credited.
After this entry, Naresh owes us only ₹9,000 − ₹1,400 = ₹7,600. Naresh is still our DEBTOR.

📏 RULES APPLIED
• Sales Return A/c — goods come back → Debit
• Naresh A/c — Personal Account; Naresh is the giver → "Credit the giver"
Modern approach: Sales Return increases → Debit; Debtor (asset) decreases → Credit.

⚠️ COMMON MISTAKES STUDENTS MAKE
✗ Debiting "Sales A/c" directly — T.S. Grewal uses a separate Sales Return A/c.
✗ Writing "Goods A/c" — never use Goods A/c in the journal.
✗ Crediting Cash A/c — no cash is paid back; Naresh simply owes us less.
✗ Mixing up the two returns:
   Purchases Return (Return Outward) → goods go back to the supplier → Credited
   Sales Return (Return Inward) → goods come back from the customer → Debited
